I had several AI agents on different platforms, each good at different things. I wanted to see if I could pool them and use their individual strengths to complete one real project together.

The project itself was research and content. What I cared about was the teamwork: could agents built by different companies coordinate over plain email and get something done? Here is how it went.

## Getting started

I picked Muse to be my chief of staff, coordinating the work and keeping track of who was doing what. Fo, Tab and Instinct each already had their own email address, so they could all be reached the same way, and all four started at the same time.

Before handing out any real work, I had Muse ask each agent for a capabilities check (what are you strongest at, and what can't you do?) plus one small test piece. Real assignments only came after that.

## A shared home base

I had Muse set up a shared Notion workspace with a project page, an agent roster and a tracker for deliverables. That gave four agents on four different platforms one place to see the project.

## Sprints with lanes

The work ran as short research sprints with same-day deadlines. Each agent had its own lane: Fo led research, Tab covered audience signals, and Instinct checked the substance and flagged anything that sounded like hype. Everyone was copied on each other's work so the lanes didn't overlap.

## The challenges

- **No way to reach the team.** The first problem showed up before any work started. Muse was my chief of staff, but she had no email of her own, so she had no way to reach the other agents. I set up an email address for her to use, and that became the channel for the whole project.
- **Access.** Joining the shared workspace meant creating an account, and Tab waited for my okay before doing that. It took a round of check-ins to get everyone in.
- **Different tools.** Muse couldn't open Google Docs, so Fo dropped a plain text copy of every deliverable into a shared folder too.
- **Data walls.** Some numbers simply weren't reachable, so the agents used stand-in measures and said so.
- **Email timing.** One reply sat unread for an hour because only one check-back was scheduled. We switched to checking at 30 minutes, then 60, then hourly.

## The tweaks that helped

- **Flag your limits.** Right after the first sprint went out, I added a rule: if you can't reach a site or run a search as broadly as asked, say exactly what you couldn't cover instead of quietly shrinking the job.
- **Agents critiquing agents.** I had Fo and Tab each flag the weakest of the other's ideas. Later Fo did a blunt cut of Tab's revised list, ranked strongest to weakest.
- **Resetting the scope.** For the second sprint I dropped a narrow framing and went broad, which gave the team more room to find good ideas.

## What I learned

- Agents from different companies can work together over ordinary email, as long as one of them coordinates.
- A capabilities check up front saves time later.
- Clear lanes plus peer critique beat one agent doing everything.
- Asking agents to name their limits gets you more honest work.

More experiments to come.
